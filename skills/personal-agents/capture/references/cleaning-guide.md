# Cleaning guide

The aim is "what they meant, written down tidily". Not shorter for its own sake, not longer, not nicer.

## Fix

| Raw | Cleaned |
| --- | --- |
| "so um the the meeting went fine I think" | "The meeting went fine, I think." |
| "talk to Priya about the road map" (dictated "rode map") | "Talk to Priya about the roadmap." |
| Run-on dictation with no punctuation | Sentences and paragraphs at natural breaks |
| The same point said twice in a row | Said once |

## Keep

- Uncertainty: "I think", "not sure", "probably".
- Strong language and humour, unless the user asks otherwise.
- Numbers, names, prices and dates exactly as said.
- Reasoning: the "because" is often what they'll want later.
- Contradictions within the note. They may be the point.

## Don't add

- Emotional readings ("You seem stressed").
- Conclusions or lessons they didn't state.
- Headings, tags or structure a short note doesn't need.
- Advice, next steps or questions.

## Task, idea or done

| They said | Save as |
| --- | --- |
| "I need to renew my passport before March" | `- [ ] Renew passport (before March, as stated)` |
| "Maybe I should look at a new bank" | Plain text idea. No checkbox. |
| "Could be worth asking Tom about the shed" | Plain text idea. |
| "Done the tax return, finally" | Plain text update. If an open task for it exists, tick it and add the date. |
| "Still haven't done the tax return" | Plain text. The task stays open; don't tick or re-add it. |

## Dates

- Capture date: the date you're saving it (in the user's time zone if known).
- Event date: label it when it differs. "Yesterday's interview went well" captured on 11 October → "Event date: 10 October 2026."
- Relative dates with no anchor ("the week after the trip") stay as said.

## Ambiguity

- Unclear name: keep it and mark it, e.g. "[unclear: Jon or John]".
- Ask only if guessing would change the meaning of what's saved (for example, which of two projects a decision applies to).
