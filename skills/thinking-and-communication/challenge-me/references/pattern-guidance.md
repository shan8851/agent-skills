# Pattern Guidance

What to focus on for each kind of proposal, plus detail for the competitor check.

## Product ideas

Focus on the target user, a painful use case they hit repeatedly, competitor and substitute pressure, a credible reason to switch, the wedge, v1 scope, the proof path, how people will find it, and what not to build.

When the idea sits on top of an existing tool, don't accept the proposed layer at face value. Ask what the existing tool already guarantees, then find the missing piece. Strong reframes often move from a generic layer (wrapper, dashboard, marketplace) to the specific moment the user needs help: a decision, an approval, a check, or a record.

If the broad market is mature, say so directly. Then narrow to an underserved user or use case, reposition it as internal tooling the user would use themselves, or park it. Don't recommend a paid path unless the wedge survives competitor pressure.

## Engineering designs

Focus on domain language, module boundaries, deep modules (simple interface, real hidden complexity), public interfaces, state transitions, failure modes, test strategy, and which decisions are hard to reverse.

## Workflows and processes

Focus on the bottleneck, where a human must stay in the loop, the rollback path, maintenance overhead, the metric that should improve, and where automation would do harm.

## Writing and content

Focus on the central claim, the audience's problem, proof and examples, the obvious counterargument, what the reader should do next, and what makes it more than generic.

## Competitor check

Run this for anything that could become a product, public tool, app or paid service. Keep it compact: a few examples and a blunt implication, not a market report.

### How to check

- If web access is available, search for direct competitors and recent alternatives, and link what you find.
- If not, list what you know from general knowledge, mark it **unverified**, and say what the user should search for.
- Never invent product names, prices or features.

### What to check

- Direct competitors solving the same job.
- Substitutes people already pay for or already have installed.
- Open-source or self-hosted alternatives.
- Platform features that could absorb the idea.
- Pricing and free-tier reality, if it might be paid.
- What users would switch from, and why now.

### What to say

- **Competitive reality:** crowded, fragmented, immature, ignored or open.
- **Wedge:** what is materially different, not just nicer UI.
- **Can it be won?** Say plainly if incumbents, distribution, willingness to pay or a weak wedge make it unwinnable as framed.
- **Reframe:** if the broad idea is weak, name the narrower user or use case where it might still win.
