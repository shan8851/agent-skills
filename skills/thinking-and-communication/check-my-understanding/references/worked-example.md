# Worked examples

## 1. Quick check on a letter

Source: the fictional Northbridge Council Tax Reduction review letter (evidence by 31 October 2026, or the reduction "may be suspended from 1 November 2026").

**Agent:** Before you send anything: in your own words, what do they want, and what happens if you don't do it?

**User:** They want my payslips and a bank statement by the end of October, or they'll stop my discount.

**Agent:** You've got the deadline and the risk. One important bit to check: the letter says "a bank statement covering the last two months for **every** account held by you **or your partner**". Who does that cover in your case?

**User:** Oh, so my partner's account too, not just mine.

**Agent:** Exactly, both of you, every account. One more thing: what counts as "by the end of October" here?

**User:** Posting it by the 31st?

**Agent:** Close, but the letter says it must be "**received** no later than 31 October". If you post it on the 31st, it'll probably arrive late. Uploading online avoids that. Quick scenario: your latest payslip won't arrive until 29 October. What would you do?

**User:** Upload the rest now and send the last payslip as soon as I get it? Or ask them first.

**Agent:** Both sensible. Asking first is safer, since the letter doesn't say whether partial evidence counts.

**You've got:** what they want (payslips, statements, changes to adults living there), the 31 October deadline, and that the discount could stop.
**Still fuzzy:** nothing, though the letter doesn't say whether a partial upload counts, so it's worth asking.
**One thing to remember:** it has to *arrive* by 31 October, including your partner's statements.

Notes: one gap at a time, highest stakes first (partner's accounts, then "received"). The correction quotes the source. The scenario tests applying it, not repeating it.

## 2. Quick check on a concept (no document)

Topic: "What's the difference between a fixed-rate and a tracker mortgage?" The agent states up front that it's using general knowledge and that specifics depend on the lender.

**User:** Fixed means the rate never changes, tracker means it follows the Bank of England.

**Agent:** The tracker part's right: it moves with a reference rate, usually the Bank of England base rate, plus a set margin. On fixed: "never changes" for how long?

**User:** For the fixed period, then it goes to something else?

**Agent:** Yes. After the fixed term ends (often 2 or 5 years) it usually moves to the lender's standard variable rate unless you remortgage.

The session closes with the three-line summary and one line suggesting the user check their own offer document for the exact terms.
