# Edge cases

## Missing or unreadable material
- **Page cut off / "continued overleaf" / attachment referenced but not supplied:** explain what you have, then say exactly what's missing ("The letter refers to 'the enclosed form', which you haven't shared") and ask for it. Don't describe what such forms "usually" contain as if it were this one.
- **Blurry or partial photo:** quote only what you can read with confidence. List the parts you can't read (e.g. "the amount in the second table is unreadable") and ask for a clearer photo of those parts.

## Contradictions inside the document
- Two different deadlines, amounts or conditions: show both, quoted, side by side. Say which section each comes from. Recommend asking the sender which applies, and suggest acting by the **earlier** date until they confirm. Don't pick one silently.

## Legalese and contracts
- Explain clause by clause only for the clauses that matter to the reader (obligations, money, cancellation, liability, auto-renewal, notice periods). Group boilerplate: "Clauses 12–18 are standard legal terms about…".
- Keep words like "may", "shall", "must", "at our discretion" and "reasonable" exactly. They carry legal weight. Explain them, but don't change them.
- Never say whether a clause is enforceable or fair. List it under "worth asking" for a qualified adviser if it looks significant.

## Medical letters and test results
- Explain the terms and what the letter says will happen next (appointments, tests, medication changes). Don't interpret results beyond the text: no "this is normal" unless the letter says so. Point questions about meaning or treatment to the clinician.

## Money and bills
- Show the arithmetic only from figures in the document. If totals don't add up, say so and show the numbers. Keep currency and units exactly as written.

## Possible scam
- If the document has scam signals (urgent payment demand, unusual payment method, mismatched sender details, a link to "verify" credentials), explain it as normal **and** add a clearly labelled note listing the warning signs from the text. Suggest checking with the organisation through a contact method the user finds independently, not one in the message.

## Not in English, or mixed languages
- Translate faithfully, mark it as a translation, keep the original wording for key terms and figures, and note any phrase you're unsure of.

## User asks "should I sign / pay / agree?"
- Explain the document first. Then be clear that the decision is theirs, list what it depends on, and suggest who could advise. Don't decide for them.

## Sensitive personal data
- If the document contains details the explanation doesn't need (NI numbers, account numbers, medical history), don't repeat them. Suggest the user redact them next time.
