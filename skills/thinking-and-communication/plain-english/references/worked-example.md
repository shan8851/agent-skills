# Worked example: council letter

## Source (fictional)

> **Northbridge City Council: Council Tax Reduction Scheme**
> Ref: CTR/88431/B
> 3 October 2026
>
> Dear Resident,
>
> **Review of your Council Tax Reduction**
>
> As part of our periodic review, we need to confirm your household circumstances. Please provide the following evidence: (a) your three most recent payslips or a statement of earnings, (b) a bank statement covering the last two months for every account held by you or your partner, and (c) confirmation of any changes to the number of adults resident at the property since 1 April 2026.
>
> This information must be received no later than 31 October 2026. If we do not receive it by this date, your reduction may be suspended from 1 November 2026 and you may be liable for the full Council Tax charge for the period from the date of suspension.
>
> You can upload documents at northbridge.gov.uk/ctr-evidence or post them to the address overleaf. Please quote your reference number on all correspondence.
>
> If your circumstances have changed, you are required to notify us within 21 days of the change.

## Good output

**In short:** This is a routine check from Northbridge City Council on the discount you get on your Council Tax. They want proof of your income and who lives with you, so they can keep giving you the discount.

**What you need to do**
- Send three things by **31 October 2026** ("must be received no later than 31 October 2026"):
  1. Your **three most recent payslips** (or a statement of earnings) ("(a)")
  2. **Bank statements covering the last two months** for **every account** you or your partner have ("(b)")
  3. Confirmation of any **change in the number of adults living with you since 1 April 2026** ("(c)"). If nothing has changed, it's worth saying so.
- Quote reference **CTR/88431/B** on everything you send.
- Send it by uploading at **northbridge.gov.uk/ctr-evidence** or by post (the address is "overleaf", on the back of the letter).

**Key points**
- If they don't get it in time, your discount "may be suspended from 1 November 2026" and you "may be liable for the full Council Tax charge". In other words, you could have to pay the full amount from then.
- Note it says "received", not "sent". Posting it on the 31st may be too late.
- Separately, you have to tell them about any change in circumstances "within 21 days of the change".

**Words explained**
- **Council Tax Reduction:** a discount on your Council Tax for people on lower incomes.
- **Liable for:** you would have to pay it.
- **Suspended:** stopped, possibly temporarily.

**Not clear / worth asking**
- "May be suspended" doesn't say whether this is automatic. If you can't get everything in time, ask the council whether you can have more time.
- It doesn't say whether a payslip *and* a statement of earnings are acceptable alternatives for everyone, for example if you're self-employed. If that's you, ask what they accept instead.
- The postal address isn't in the text you shared (it's "overleaf"), so check the back of the letter.

## Why this is good

- Dates, the reference number, the URL and amounts are copied exactly.
- "May" stays "may". There's no claim the suspension is certain.
- "Received" versus "sent" is a real-world trap, flagged from the text.
- The missing postal address is flagged, not invented.
- There's no advice about entitlement or appeal rights, because the letter doesn't mention them.
