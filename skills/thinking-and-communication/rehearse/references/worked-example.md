# Worked example: asking for a pay rise

## Setup (condensed)

- **Goal:** move from £52k to £60k. Would accept £56k plus a review in 6 months. Walk-away: start looking elsewhere, with no ultimatum in the meeting.
- **Other person:** line manager Priya. Supportive but budget-constrained. Usually says "let me take it to HR".
- **Evidence:** led the payments migration (zero incidents), now mentoring two juniors, and peers in similar roles are advertised at £58–65k (the user's own research, to verify).
- **Format:** a 1:1 video call. **Difficulty:** realistic.

## Prep card (given to the user)

**Goal:** £60k · **Would accept:** £56k + 6-month review · **Walk-away:** start looking; no ultimatum.
**Opening line:** "I'd like to talk about my salary. Over the last year my role has grown quite a bit, and I'd like my pay to reflect that."
**Key points:**
1. Payments migration: led it end to end, zero incidents.
2. Scope grew: mentoring two juniors.
3. Market: similar roles are advertised at £58–65k (bring three links).
**Expect them to say:** "The budget's fixed this year." · "Let me take it to HR." · "You're already at the top of your band."

## Rehearsal excerpt

> **Priya (simulated):** Thanks for raising it. Honestly, budgets are pretty locked for this year.
> **User:** Oh, right. Okay, I understand, maybe next year then?
> **User:** time out
> **Coach:** You conceded in the first exchange. "Budgets are locked" is an opening position, not a final answer. Try acknowledging it, then restating the ask and asking about the process: "I understand budgets are tight. Given how the role's changed, I'd still like us to look at it properly. What would the process be to get this reviewed?" Want to resume from her line?
> **User:** yes
> **Priya (simulated):** Thanks for raising it. Honestly, budgets are pretty locked for this year.
> **User:** I understand it's tight. Given the migration and the mentoring, I'd still like us to look at it properly. What's the process for getting it reviewed?
> **Priya (simulated):** I can take it to HR, but I can't promise anything. What number did you have in mind?

## Debrief

**What landed:** "Given the migration and the mentoring, I'd still like us to look at it properly." It was calm, specific, and kept the conversation open.
**Where it slipped:** the first run, "maybe next year then?", gave away the ask before she'd said no.
**Try instead:** when asked for a number: "Based on the scope now and what similar roles pay, I'm looking for £60k." Then stop talking.
**If they say…**
- "Top of your band": "Then I'd like to understand what moving to the next level looks like, and on what timeline."
- "Let me take it to HR": "Great. Could we agree to pick it back up by [date]?"
**Before the real thing:** check the three salary links are current, write the migration outcome in one sentence, and practise saying the number out loud.

## Why this is good

- The simulated manager was plausible: one realistic objection, no instant cave.
- The time-out gave one concrete better line, not a lecture.
- The debrief quotes the user's actual words and gives lines in their voice.
- The salary research is flagged as the user's own, to verify, not presented as fact.
